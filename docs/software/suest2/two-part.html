Two-part models

Expected outcomes from a model of whether the outcome is positive and a model of how large it is

A two-part model is used when an outcome is the product of a mix of two processes, one for observed zeroes and non-zeroes, and one for an amount among the non-zeroes. The expected outcome is the product of the two:

\[E(y) = \Pr(y > 0) \times E(y \mid y > 0)\]

This is the model the twopm command fits (Belotti, Deb, Manning, and Norton 2015). With suest2, fit the two parts as separate models, combine them, and give margins the product of the two predictions. The combined results also give effects within each part and comparisons across groups.

The examples use the medical expenditures of 19,386 adults in the 2004 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey. The first part is a logit of whether a person had any medical expenditures. The second part is a Poisson model of expenditures among the people who had any. With robust standard errors, poisson is Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood: it fits a model of the mean of any outcome that is zero or positive, not only counts, and needs only the model of the mean to be right (Santos Silva and Tenreyro 2006).

Fit the two parts and combine them

Both parts cluster on the dwelling unit (duid):

use "https://tdmize.github.io/data/data/meps", clear
(MEPS 2004 adults, total medical expenditures (from R package twopartm))
generate byte any = exp_tot > 0
tabulate any
        any |      Freq.     Percent        Cum.
------------+-----------------------------------
          0 |      3,440       17.74       17.74
          1 |     15,946       82.26      100.00
------------+-----------------------------------
      Total |     19,386      100.00
local x "c.age i.female c.lninc c.pcs12 c.mcs12 i.ins_unins i.anylim"
quietly logit any `x', vce(cluster duid)
estimates store Any
quietly poisson exp_tot `x' if any, vce(cluster duid)
estimates store Amount
suest2 Any Amount

With survey data, fit both parts with svy: and the second part with if any, not subpop(): suest2 needs the same subpopulation in every model, and it computes the standard errors from the full survey design.

How well the model predicts

The correlation between the outcome and its expected value summarizes how well a two-part model predicts. It depends only on the expected outcome, so it applies with any second part:

predict double yhat if e(sample), twopart(Any Amount)
correlate exp_tot yhat
(obs=19,386)

             |  exp_tot     yhat
-------------+------------------
     exp_tot |   1.0000
        yhat |   0.3916   1.0000

The correlation is .392.

Other second parts and comparisons

The second part can also be a glm, such as a gamma family with a log link, regress, or nbreg; predict(twopart()) works the same way with each. With a regression of the log of the outcome, add duan for Duan’s (1983) smearing estimate or normal for the normal-theory retransformation, as in predict(twopart(Any LnAmount) duan). The mecompare Two-part models page compares effects across two-part models with different second parts, and between a two-part model and a single model for the outcome.
