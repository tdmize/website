Two-part models

Expected outcomes from a model of whether the outcome is positive and a model of how large it is

Outcomes such as medical expenditures are zero for many people and spread over a long right tail for the rest. A two-part model handles them in two steps: a binary model of whether the outcome is positive, and a model of its size for the people with a positive outcome. The expected outcome is the product of the two:

\[E(y) = \Pr(y > 0) \times E(y \mid y > 0)\]

This is the model the twopm command fits (Belotti, Deb, Manning, and Norton 2015). With suest2, fit the two parts as separate models, combine them, and give margins the product of the two predictions in expression(). Each predict() names its model with model(), so the same combined results also give effects within each part, comparisons across groups, and comparisons across two-part models.

The examples use the medical expenditures of 19,386 adults in the 2004 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey.

Fit the two parts and combine them

The first part is a logit of whether a person had any medical expenditures. The second part is a glm with a gamma family and a log link, fit to the people who had any. Both cluster on the dwelling unit (duid).

use "https://tdmize.github.io/data/data/meps", clear
(MEPS 2004 adults, total medical expenditures (from R package twopartm))
generate byte any = exp_tot > 0
tabulate any
        any |      Freq.     Percent        Cum.
------------+-----------------------------------
          0 |      3,440       17.74       17.74
          1 |     15,946       82.26      100.00
------------+-----------------------------------
      Total |     19,386      100.00
local x "c.age i.female c.lninc c.pcs12 c.mcs12 i.ins_unins i.anylim"
quietly logit any `x', vce(cluster duid)
estimates store Any
quietly glm exp_tot `x' if any, family(gamma) link(log) vce(cluster duid)

Other models for the second part

A linear second part. Fit regress among the people with any expenditures, and use its prediction in place of the glm one:

local x "c.age i.female c.lninc c.pcs12 c.mcs12 i.ins_unins i.anylim"
quietly regress exp_tot `x' if any, vce(cluster duid)
estimates store Linear
quietly suest2 Any Linear
margins, dydx(ins_unins) expression(predict(model(Any)) * predict(model(Linear)))
Average marginal effects                                Number of obs = 19,386
Model VCE: Robust

Expression: predict(model(Any)) * predict(model(Linear))
dy/dx wrt:  1.ins_unins

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
             |            Delta-method
             |      dy/dx   std. err.      z    P>|z|     [95% conf. interval]
-------------+----------------------------------------------------------------
 1.ins_unins |  -2287.542    109.406  -20.909   0.000    -2501.975   -2073.110
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A regression of log expenditures. When the second part is a regression of the log of the outcome, its prediction is on the log scale. Exponentiating it gives exp(xb), which understates the mean; the expected outcome is exp(xb) times the mean of the exponentiated errors. Two estimates of that constant are common: Duan’s (1983) smearing estimate, the mean of the exponentiated residuals, and, if the errors are normal, exp(s²/2), where s² is the residual variance. Compute either one from the stored regression and include it in expression():

local x "c.age i.female c.lninc c.pcs12 c.mcs12 i.ins_unins i.anylim"
generate double lnexp = ln(exp_tot) if any
(3,440 missing values generated)
quietly regress lnexp `x' if any, vce(cluster duid)
estimates store LnAmount
local s2 = e(rss) / e(N)
predict double res if e(sample), residuals
(3,440 missing values generated)
generate double expres = exp(res)
(3,440 missing values generated)
summarize expres, meanonly
local duan = r(mean)
display "Smearing constant: " %5.3f `duan' _n "Normal constant:   " %5.3f exp(`s2' / 2)
Smearing constant: 2.401
Normal constant:   2.586
quietly suest2 Any LnAmount
margins, dydx(ins_unins) expression(predict(model(Any)) * exp(predict(model(LnAmount))) * `duan')
Average marginal effects                                Number of obs = 19,386
Model VCE: Robust

Expression: predict(model(Any)) * exp(predict(model(LnAmount))) * 2.400751486817317

Weights and survey data

Sampling weights. Fit each part with the weights but without vce(), and give the clustering to suest2 with cluster():

local x "c.age i.female c.lninc c.pcs12 c.mcs12 i.ins_unins i.anylim"
quietly logit any `x' [pw = wtdper]
estimates store AnyW
quietly glm exp_tot `x' if any [pw = wtdper], family(gamma) link(log)
estimates store AmountW
quietly suest2 AnyW AmountW, cluster(duid)
margins, dydx(ins_unins) expression(predict(model(AnyW)) * predict(model(AmountW)))

Comparing two two-part models

suest2 can combine more than one two-part model. Here a two-part model without the health measures (pcs12, mcs12, and anylim) is combined with the full one, to ask how much the effect of being uninsured changes when health is held constant. The difference between the two expected outcomes goes in one expression():

local x1 "c.age i.female c.lninc i.ins_unins"
quietly logit any `x1', vce(cluster duid)
estimates store Any1
quietly glm exp_tot `x1' if any, family(gamma) link(log) vce(cluster duid)
estimates store Amount1
quietly suest2 Any1 Amount1 Any Amount
local tp1 "predict(model(Any1)) * predict(model(Amount1))"
local tp2 "predict(model(Any)) * predict(model(Amount))"
margins, dydx(ins_unins) expression(`tp1')
